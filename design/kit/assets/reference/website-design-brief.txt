Growing stronger every day.

About This Brief
This brief pulls together everything the website build needs from Power ABA’s brand system — colors, type, logo usage, character rules, voice, and accessibility standards — sized to read start to finish rather than mined out of a longer internal document.
It’s excerpted from the full Poppy &amp; Parker Brand Bible (v1.3, Internal: Marketing &amp; Design). If a question comes up that isn’t covered here, ask and we’ll pull the relevant page.
Everything in this brief is launch scope for the website: navigation, a resource center, service explanations, FAQs, and character guideposts built on the visual and voice rules below. One thing it is not: the “Power Pals Adventures” story hub referenced elsewhere in the brand system is a longer-term growth vision, paired with a future YouTube Kids channel — not part of this build. If anything here reads like a request for story-hub content, flag it and we’ll confirm scope together.

Make Power ABA feel welcoming, understandable, hopeful, and memorable — supporting, not replacing, the expertise of clinicians and caregivers.

For families navigating learning, communication, and everyday routines, Poppy and Parker are friendly Power ABA guides who turn practical support into hopeful, memorable stories—because growth happens one meaningful step at a time.

Illustration Style
Polished 2D animation with clean linework, expressive faces, vivid but controlled color, and soft dimensional shading.
Friendly proportions and movement without exaggerated babyish styling.
Simple, uncluttered backgrounds — let the message carry the page, not the scene.
The colorful flower badge is integrated into the red shield as part of the costume, never pasted over it.
Consistent facial structure, hair, glasses, clothing, badge orientation, and relative height across every asset used on-site.
Composition &amp; Asset Use
Do
Avoid
Give the characters breathing room and a clear focal point.
Crowding characters, text, icons, and props into one frame.
Use clean crops that preserve faces, hands, badges, and gestures.
Cropping through eyes, hands, badges, or important communication tools.
Place characters beside the message rather than filling the frame.
Making every page look like a children’s cartoon scene.
Use simple backgrounds so the message stays legible.
Busy patterns or sensory-heavy backgrounds that compete with the message.
Maintain consistent lighting, linework, proportions, and color.
Mixing unrelated illustration styles on the same page.

Always sound
Warm, encouraging, plainspoken, patient, respectful, practical, hopeful, and strengths-based.
Never sound

05
Accessibility Standards
Not optional, and not brand flavor — build to this spec.

Area
Standard
Captions
Caption all spoken audio; edit auto-captions for accuracy.
Contrast
Use high-contrast text and do not rely on color alone to communicate meaning.
Motion
Avoid rapid flashing, unnecessary motion, and overstimulating transitions.
Text
Use readable type sizes, short paragraphs, descriptive headings, and plain language.
Images
Write meaningful alt text that describes the purpose of the image.
Audio
Keep music below speech and avoid sudden loud effects.
Downloads
Create accessible PDFs and printables with logical reading order and labels.

Before publishing anything that collects information from children or links to child-directed video, review current platform rules and applicable legal requirements with qualified counsel and compliance leadership.
