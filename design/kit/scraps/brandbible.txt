Positioning Statement
Positioning
For families navigating learning, communication, and everyday routines, Poppy and Parker are friendly Power ABA guides who turn practical support into hopeful, memorable stories—because growth happens one meaningful step at a time.

Two-Lane Content Model
Lane A: Family Guidance
The primary lane. Parent-focused headlines, clinician-backed tips, downloadable resources, caregiver encouragement, service education, and community updates.
Lane B: Power Pals Adventures
A clearly labeled child-friendly series. Short stories, routines, preparation videos, simple coping tools, songs, and interactive prompts.

What the Power Pals Are Not
They are not diagnostic tools, clinical authorities, or substitutes for individualized care.
They are not “autism superheroes” who overcome, cure, defeat, or erase autism.
They are not a promise that effort always produces a specific outcome.
They are not used to compare one child’s pace or progress with another child’s.

Character Profiles
Distinct personalities that work best as a supportive team.

Poppy
Curious, creative, expressive, optimistic, and full of ideas. Poppy loves exploring possibilities. When her first plan does not work, she practices flexibility and looks for another path.Signature phrase: “Let’s find another way!”
Parker
Thoughtful, observant, caring, patient, and determined. Parker notices details and reminds others that courage can mean taking one manageable step or asking for support.Signature phrase: “One step at a time!”

Poppy’s Story Function
Introduces curiosity, creativity, communication, flexibility, and trying a new approach.
Asks open questions and offers choices rather than rushing to a solution.
Sometimes moves too quickly or becomes frustrated, giving her room to practice pausing and adapting.
Parker’s Story Function
Introduces persistence, patience, self-advocacy, calm strategies, and step-by-step planning.
Notices feelings, details, and environmental needs that others may miss.
Sometimes feels uncertain in new situations, giving him room to model preparation and supported courage.
Their Friendship
Poppy and Parker do not complete each other because either one is missing something. They complement each other because different strengths create more possibilities. Sometimes Poppy helps Parker try; sometimes Parker helps Poppy pause. They ask, listen, practice, and celebrate together.
Relationship rule
Neither character is always the teacher, the brave one, the calm one, or the person who needs help. Their roles should change naturally from story to story.

07
Social Media Strategy
Use the characters as trusted guides for adults and encouraging friends for children.

Strategic Role
Poppy and Parker should make Power ABA’s social content easier to recognize, understand, save, and share. They are not decorative mascots placed on every graphic. Each appearance should serve a communication purpose: introduce a topic, model a strategy, frame a story, celebrate a strength, or guide a next step.
How Often to Feature Poppy &amp; Parker
Aim for the illustrated characters appearing in roughly two out of every three posts — not every post, and not a fixed rotation. The Power Bloom mark, color palette, and “Growing stronger every day” can carry brand identity on any post, including the ones where Poppy and Parker sit out.
Monday (Parker’s One-Step Plan): Parker, solo. The series name already ties this to his strength.
Wednesday (Power Pal Tip or Mini-Adventure): the swing day. For a tip, let the message pick the character — Poppy for flexibility/trying a new approach, Parker for patience/step-by-step planning. For a Mini-Adventure, use both together, since the story is about their friendship.
Friday (Power Bloom Friday): often neither. Real staff photos, caregiver quotes, or behind-the-scenes moments work well here, carried by the Power Bloom mark and color system rather than the characters.
Default to solo appearances over showing both together. Solo appearances let which character appears signal what the post is about — pairing them on every post erases that shorthand. Reserve “both together” for Mini-Adventures, content specifically about their friendship, or brand-anchor materials (cover art, Quick Reference) meant to represent the whole brand rather than one specific tip. The one rule to hold firm: never default to showing both simply out of habit.

08
In-Clinic Culture
Ways the Power Bloom can grow beyond content, into everyday culture at Power ABA.
The Power Bloom doesn’t have to live only in social posts and stories — it can become something children, families, and staff experience in the clinic itself.
The Power Wall Garden
Each child works toward their own Power Bloom — a blank flower with five petal outlines for Courage, Curiosity, Kindness, Calm &amp; Confidence, and Self-Expression. Petals are filled in whenever staff notice one of those qualities show up, individualized to what matters for that child rather than a fixed checklist. Once all five petals are earned, the flower moves to a shared Power Wall — a growing display in the clinic that becomes more alive with every child who completes one.
Petals are never removed once earned, and the wall shows no pace or ranking — it’s a garden the whole practice grows together, not a leaderboard.
The Seed Ritual
Every new client receives their first blank Power Bloom during their very first session, presented as a gift from Poppy and Parker. It’s a small moment that makes the framework part of the relationship from day one, rather than something introduced later.
The Petal Log
A simple take-home card or magnet lets caregivers note petal moments they notice at home — trying a new food, using a communication device unprompted, asking for a break independently. Those moments feed into the same flower as the ones earned in clinic, giving caregivers a tangible way to take part in their child’s progress.
Power Bloom Friday (In-Clinic)
Distinct from the Power Bloom Friday social content series: a short weekly ritual — a closing circle at the end of the week where children and staff each share one bloom moment. It costs nothing to run and keeps the Power Bloom language alive in daily practice, not just on social media.
Shared Clinical Language
Staff begin using the five petal words — courage, curiosity, kindness, calm and confidence, self-expression — in session notes and caregiver updates in place of clinical jargon. It keeps the brand’s voice consistent everywhere a family encounters Power ABA, not just in marketing materials.

Story World &amp; Narrative System

A lane to build into, not a launch requirement

The World: Power Place
Poppy and Parker live in a bright, welcoming community called Power Place. It includes familiar environments such as home, school, the playground, the store, the dentist, birthday parties, therapy sessions, and community events. The world feels imaginative, but the situations stay grounded in real family life.
The Power Bloom
The colorful flower on their badges is called the Power Bloom. It does not glow because someone was perfect. It brightens when a character communicates, practices, asks for help, shows kindness, tries a strategy, or celebrates meaningful progress. Per the approved reference artwork, the five petals are arranged clockwise from the top point in this fixed order: Yellow, Orange, Coral, Blue, Purple. This order is locked and should never be redrawn or rearranged.

Six-Beat Story Formula
1.  A familiar challenge appears: something is new, frustrating, confusing, loud, delayed, or different than expected.
2.  A character notices and names the experience without judgment: “This feels hard right now.”
3.  They choose a Power Tool: a visual, a choice, a break, a question, a first-then plan, practice, or trusted support.
4.  They try. The first attempt does not need to be perfect or complete.
5.  They identify meaningful progress: communicating, participating, asking for help, trying one step, or returning when ready.
6.  The Power Bloom responds and the story closes with: “We’re growing stronger every day!”
Origin Story
Poppy once believed superheroes had to be fearless. Parker believed superheroes always knew exactly what to do. When they faced a challenge neither could solve alone, Poppy became frustrated and Parker felt overwhelmed. They paused, explained what they needed, asked for help, and broke the challenge into smaller steps. Safeguards &amp; Accessibility
Protect families, preserve dignity, and make every asset easier to use.

Privacy and Story Use
Use fictional, composite, or generalized scenarios unless documented permission and compliance approval are in place.
Never recreate a recognizable client story, routine, location, or outcome without appropriate authorization.
Do not reveal protected or sensitive information through captions, background details, screenshots, or metadata.
Keep testimonials and client images within approved legal, clinical, and organizational processes.
Clinical and Ethical Guardrails
Avoid guaranteed results, universal timelines, and one-size-fits-all recommendations.
Use respectful, individualized language and recognize multiple forms of communication.
Do not frame autism, disability, emotion, or sensory needs as villains to defeat.
Do not use fear, guilt, or urgency to drive inquiries.
Make clear when content is general education rather than individualized clinical guidance.
Have qualified professionals review clinical explanations before publication.
Accessibility Standards
Area
Standard
Captions
Caption all spoken audio; edit auto-captions for accuracy.
Contrast
Use high-contrast text and do not rely on color alone to communicate meaning.
Motion
Avoid rapid flashing, unnecessary motion, and overstimulating transitions.
Text
Use readable type sizes, short paragraphs, descriptive headings, and plain language.
Images
Write meaningful alt text that describes the purpose of the image.
Audio
Keep music below speech and avoid sudden loud effects.
Downloads
Create accessible PDFs and printables with logical reading order and labels.

Review reminder
Before launching child-directed video or collecting information from children, review current platform rules and applicable legal requirements with qualified counsel and compliance leadership.

Launch Roadmap &amp; Measures
Build the system in phases, test it with families, and improve it deliberately.

Phase 1: Foundation
Finalize character sheets, flower badge, color files, and social templates.
Clear mascot names, tagline, artwork ownership, and relevant trademark usage.
Create a review workflow involving brand, clinical, compliance, and accessibility owners.
Produce a core library of 20–30 static poses and expressions.
Phase 2: Soft Launch
Introduce Poppy and Parker through one pinned post, one short video, and a website section.
Launch three recurring series: Power Pal Tips, Parker’s One-Step Plan, and Power Bloom Friday.
Test adult-facing content first and gather feedback from caregivers and staff.
Phase 3: Story Expansion
Release the first Power Pals Mini-Adventure.
Create printables tied to stories and social posts.
Develop a searchable video playlist and email content stream.
Add seasonal and community story environments without changing the core character designs.
